01

Scope and assumptions

This proposal covers a thirty-day pilot on twelve cameras in one contiguous zone of the mall, with a defined path to estate-wide rollout after review. It deliberately does not price or commit to the full estate before the pilot has produced measured numbers.

  • The mall operates an existing NVR or DVR recording the cameras in scope, reachable over a wired LAN.
  • Cameras expose RTSP, either via ONVIF discovery or a per-brand URL we configure manually.
  • Existing recording, retention and camera hardware are not modified. Nightwatch is an additional read-only consumer of the streams.
  • The control room has an available wired network port and mains power for one 1U or small-form-factor appliance.
  • A general-purpose internet connection is available. A dedicated line is not required.
02

What gets installed

A single appliance — the "edge box" — placed in the control room or server room. It runs the full detection pipeline locally. There is no software to install on any of the mall's existing machines and no agent on operator workstations.

Sizing, stated honestly

One appliance covers twelve cameras today. An estate of 300 cameras is therefore not one box — it is a fleet, and the per-box density is the single most important number the pilot needs to establish. We would rather measure it on your cameras than quote you a number from a datasheet.

03

Network requirements

Inbound to your network

None

No port forwarding. No static public IP. No inbound firewall rule. No VPN terminating inside your LAN. The appliance initiates every connection it makes.

Outbound from the appliance

HTTPS on 443, plus one persistent connection

A single long-lived encrypted connection to the Nightwatch backend carries health status and live-view setup messages. Event data and media are ordinary HTTPS uploads.

DirectionPortPurpose
Appliance → NVR/cameras (LAN)554RTSP stream read. Internal only, never leaves the LAN.
Appliance → NVR/cameras (LAN)80 / 3702ONVIF discovery and device query during setup.
Appliance → internet443Control connection, event upload, media upload, credential issuance.
Appliance → internet3478 / 5349Only when a person opens live view, for the direct browser connection. Idle otherwise.
Internet → your network—Nothing. No listening service is exposed.

Bandwidth

Nightwatch does not maintain a video uplink. Outbound traffic is proportional to the number of events, not the number of cameras. A typical event is a snapshot plus a ten-second clip — on the order of one to two megabytes. Two hundred events in a day is a few hundred megabytes. Live view consumes real bandwidth only for as long as a person has a camera open.

05

Security posture

Appliance identity

Pairing, then a bound device token

The appliance is paired once using a short-lived single-use code entered in your dashboard. It then holds a long-lived token bound to its own keypair and scoped to your organisation alone.

Standing secrets on the box

None

The appliance carries no permanent cloud service-account key and no permanent AI provider key. It exchanges its device token for short-lived credentials, each valid for at most thirty minutes, and for individually signed one-time upload URLs.

Revocation

Immediate and total

Revoking the device token from your dashboard stops event ingestion at once and stops the appliance's access to any AI or storage credential within one short expiry window. No physical access is needed.

User sessions

Server-side, bound to device

Dashboard sessions are opaque server-side tokens, encrypted at rest, bound to the originating address and browser, with idle and absolute expiry and brute-force lockout. A copied token does not work elsewhere.

Tenant isolation

Enforced per query

Every record carries your organisation identifier and every read is filtered by it in the data layer — not hidden by the user interface.

Audit

Recorded and exportable

Logins, camera views, configuration changes and administrative actions are written to an audit log available to your security head.

06

Privacy and DPDP posture

  • No facial recognition and no biometric identification. The system detects that a person is present and describes behaviour within a zone. It does not build a face database, does not match individuals against any watchlist, and cannot tell you who someone is. This is a product decision, not a configuration.
  • No cross-camera identity matching in pilot scope. Correlating a person's path between cameras is a separate and more privacy-sensitive capability that is not part of this deployment.
  • Minimisation by design. The overwhelming majority of recorded footage never leaves the premises and is never stored by us, because uneventful frames are discarded at the edge.
  • Your existing obligations are unchanged. Signage, notice, and the lawful basis for CCTV operation remain the mall's responsibility as they are today. Nightwatch adds analysis over footage you are already lawfully recording.
Open item for the pilot

A configurable retention period for events and clips is not yet a self-service setting. For the pilot we will agree a fixed retention window in writing and apply it operationally. Self-service retention configuration is on the roadmap and is called out as such rather than implied.

08

NVR and camera compatibility

Integration is at the stream level, so brand support is a question of RTSP reachability rather than vendor partnership.

  • Automatic discovery via ONVIF where the NVR or cameras support it — the common case for CP Plus, Hikvision, Dahua and their OEM rebrands.
  • Manual configuration per camera where discovery is unavailable or disabled, using the brand's documented RTSP URL pattern and a read-only account.
  • Sub-stream preferred. Where cameras expose a secondary lower-resolution stream, we consume that. It reduces load on the NVR and is sufficient for detection.
  • Read-only account requested. Nightwatch needs stream read access only. It does not need, and should not be given, camera administration rights.
Pre-install check

Before the install day we ask for the NVR make and model, the number of concurrent stream sessions it supports, and confirmation that a read-only account can be created. Concurrent session limits on older NVRs are the most common cause of a surprise on install day.

09

Failure modes

If this happensThen
Internet link dropsDetection continues uninterrupted on the appliance. Events queue locally and are delivered when the link returns. Alerts are delayed, not lost.
A camera goes offlineThe camera is marked offline in the dashboard and reported in health telemetry. Other cameras are unaffected.
The detection process crashesIt is supervised and restarted automatically. The gap is visible in health telemetry rather than silent.
The appliance loses powerThe twelve cameras on it are not analysed until it returns. Your NVR keeps recording throughout — nothing is lost from the record, only from live analysis. Appliance redundancy is not part of pilot scope.
Scene analysis is unavailable upstreamLocal detection continues and events still fire, with reduced descriptive detail. Digests fall back to a summary assembled from event metadata.
Daily AI spend cap is reachedA per-organisation cap is enforced. On reaching it, analysis degrades to local detection only for the remainder of the day and the dashboard says so plainly.

On false alarms

Expect week one to be noisy. The honest measure is not the day-one rate but whether tuning brings it down and holds it down — that is exactly what weeks one to four are for, and it is the number we would want you to judge us on.

12

Commercials and out of scope

Pilot

  • Appliance supplied on loan, returned if the pilot does not proceed.
  • Installation, configuration and training included.
  • Commercial terms for the pilot itself to be confirmed by Nightwatch before signature.

Rollout — structure, not a number

Estate pricing is deliberately not quoted here. It has two components, and one of them cannot be honestly estimated before the pilot:

  • Appliances — a function of cameras per appliance, which the pilot measures on your actual cameras and stream profiles.
  • Per-camera monthly subscription — covering analysis, alerting, dashboard access, event storage and support.

Explicitly out of scope

  • Replacing, repairing or reconfiguring existing cameras, cabling or NVR equipment.
  • Acting as a recording or archival system. Your NVR remains the system of record and the source for evidentiary footage.
  • Guarding, monitoring or response services. Nightwatch surfaces events; your team decides and acts.
  • Any capability marked roadmap in §07.
Before estate-wide rollout

Three items in §07 — per-zone account restriction, incident acknowledgement workflow, and fleet management across appliances — are genuine prerequisites for running this across a full mall estate with a shift-based control room. They are on the roadmap with the rollout in mind, and we would rather name them here than have them discovered during deployment.
